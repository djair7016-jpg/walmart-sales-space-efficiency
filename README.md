# Walmart: Sales and Space Efficiency Analysis

## Executive Summary

Walmart needed to understand whether sales volume and store space were being used efficiently across departments and store locations.

I analyzed 95,880 weekly sales records from 45 stores, with a detailed focus on 28,845 records from 2012. Using Google Sheets, I integrated store and department information, validated the data, created pivot tables, and developed KPIs such as department sales participation and sales per square meter.

The analysis showed that high sales volume did not always correspond to efficient use of space. Garden and Outdoor Living recorded the lowest identified department participation, representing 1.13% of sales, with approximately $5.96 million in revenue and $42.86 in sales per square meter.

Health and Wellness represented 9.64% of identified department sales and generated approximately $391.47 per square meter, indicating an opportunity to review its commercial space and product layout.

Based on these findings, I recommended reviewing space allocation by department, improving product layout, and evaluating resource decisions using both sales volume and spatial efficiency.

## Business Problem

The project focused on the following questions:

- Which departments generated the highest sales?
- Which departments contributed the greatest share of total revenue?
- Did departments with high sales also use store space efficiently?
- Which areas showed high volume but comparatively low sales per square meter?
- How did results vary by store type and holiday periods?

The objective was to support decisions related to store space, commercial priorities, and operational resource allocation.

## Dataset and Scope

The analysis used 95,880 historical weekly sales records from 45 Walmart stores.

The dataset included:

- Store identifier
- Department
- Weekly sales
- Store type
- Store size
- Date
- Holiday indicator

The main diagnostic focused on 28,845 records from 2012.

## Data Preparation

I used Google Sheets to:

- inspect missing and inconsistent values;
- integrate store and department catalogs;
- connect records using `VLOOKUP`;
- validate department identifiers;
- create calculated KPI fields;
- prepare pivot tables and executive summaries.

A department identifier without a corresponding catalog description was detected during the analysis. This limitation must be considered when interpreting department participation.

## KPI Development

The following indicators were created:

### Department Sales Participation

Measures the percentage of identified sales generated by each department.

### Sales per Square Meter

Measures the commercial efficiency of the space assigned to each department or store.

### Opportunity Index

Used to identify departments with meaningful sales volume but comparatively low spatial efficiency.

These indicators allowed sales volume and space utilization to be evaluated together.

## Analytical Approach

### 1. Department Performance

Departments were compared according to:

- Weekly sales
- Total sales
- Sales participation
- Sales per square meter

### 2. Store Comparison

Performance was evaluated by:

- Store
- Store type
- Store size
- Relative spatial efficiency

### 3. Time and Holiday Analysis

Weekly sales were reviewed to identify:

- Seasonal changes
- Holiday-related increases
- Periods of stronger or weaker performance

## Key Findings

- High sales volume did not always indicate efficient use of store space.
- Garden and Outdoor Living recorded the lowest identified department participation:
  - Sales participation: 1.13%
  - Total sales: approximately $5.96 million
  - Sales per square meter: approximately $42.86
- Health and Wellness represented:
  - Sales participation: 9.64%
  - Sales per square meter: approximately $391.47
- Store size alone did not guarantee stronger relative performance.
- Holiday periods produced sales increases, but the effect was not uniform across departments.
- Department-level analysis was more informative when sales participation and spatial efficiency were evaluated together.

## Business Recommendations

- Review the amount of space assigned to departments with low sales participation and low sales per square meter.
- Improve layout and product placement in departments with meaningful sales volume but lower-than-expected spatial efficiency.
- Avoid prioritizing departments using sales volume alone.
- Use sales per square meter as a recurring operational KPI.
- Compare store types before replicating space-allocation decisions.
- Plan inventory, promotions, and staffing around identified seasonal and holiday patterns.
- Correct or document unidentified department codes before using the analysis for formal allocation decisions.
